# Summary Selection

Use this reference when writing or rewriting a summary, TL;DR, executive
summary, or an opening that summarizes a larger source. Select the smallest
summary that lets the intended reader understand relevance, current outcome,
and any necessary action without reading the body. Apply the main skill's
wording, terminology, and evidence guidance to the selected content.

## Preserve the requested scope and form

Follow the user's requested content and structure, then the artifact's template
or documented convention. Otherwise choose a short paragraph or a few bullets.
Labels are optional; use the audience's vocabulary when they help scanning.
Do not impose a TL;DR template, add a summary to every artifact, or rewrite the
surrounding body when the request covers only its summary. Investigation,
review, verification, and publication belong to their own workflows.

## Select what changes the reader's decision

Lead with the conclusion or current outcome. Keep only details that change the
reader's understanding, decision, or next action. Depending on the source,
these may be the topic and relevance, result or state, important supporting
point, material risk or unknown, or a known owner and dependency.

These are content options, not required sections. Omit empty concepts rather
than writing `None`. Do not invent a problem, approach, tradeoff, evidence,
owner, deadline, or action to complete a shape. Counts, file inventories, and
implementation details belong only when they affect the reader's decision.

Possible shapes include a result and its limit, a decision with its reason and
consequence, or a finding with evidence and an unknown. Prefer a paragraph when
labels would repeat the text, and bullets for parallel facts or actions. Avoid
nested lists, tables, and code blocks unless the requested summary needs them.
There is no universal word count. One short paragraph or up to five bullets is
a useful default; the user's format and necessary meaning take precedence.

## Keep the evidence boundary visible

State established facts directly, attribute supplied claims when provenance
affects trust, and keep inferences, unknowns, and pending work distinguishable.
State what a relevant check establishes and its material limit. A component
test does not establish production behavior; "checks passed" or "all issues
are fixed" can hide that distinction.

Keep an available descriptive source reference when it helps check a
consequential claim. Do not require an external link for every supplied fact,
invent evidence, or treat an absent citation as proof that a statement is false.
Name an owner when the source identifies one and the item needs action. Mention
an unassigned owner only when that gap changes the reader's decision.

## Rewrite from the current source

1. Read the current artifact, supplied context, and relevant decisions.
2. Draft from that state, then compare the old summary to recover still-valid
   points. The old summary is not the authority.
3. Remove superseded claims, resolved questions, and obsolete owners. Update
   evidence status when a fact, inference, unknown, or blocker has changed.
4. Check that the summary and current body agree. Keep editing history in its
   intended surface unless the reader requests a chronological summary.

## Check selection separately from wording

Can a reader without prior context understand the outcome, why it concerns
them, and any necessary action? Does each remaining point affect that judgment?
Remove unsupported completion claims, empty praise, process narration, isolated
"Done" endings, and detail copied wholesale from the body. Keep meaningful
contrasts and uncertainty; brevity must not change what the source establishes.

Fictional source: the contact panel now shows custom fields. A component test
confirms display; production has not been checked. An old summary says all
issues are fixed. An editing note says the implementation changed after review.

Useful summary:

> The contact panel now shows custom fields. A component test confirms their
> display; production remains unverified.

This selects the visible result and relevant evidence limit, removes obsolete
completion and editing history, and adds no unsupported owner or next action.
